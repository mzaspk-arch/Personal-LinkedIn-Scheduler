**Risk management and internal audit are not the same job.**

Many growing businesses treat them as one function. That leaves gaps.

Here is the simple difference.

**Risk management:**
▶ **Purpose:** identify, assess and manage risks.
▶ **Timing:** proactive, before risks occur.
▶ **Owner:** the management team.
▶ **Objective:** protect the organisation from potential losses.

**Internal audit:**
▶ **Purpose:** evaluate controls and processes.
▶ **Timing:** reviews after processes are implemented.
▶ **Owner:** independent internal auditors.
▶ **Objective:** confirm that policies and controls are working properly.

Risk management prepares for uncertainty.

Internal audit ensures accountability and effectiveness.

You need both. If the same people design the controls and then check them, nobody is giving you an independent view.

✅ Keep a short risk register owned by management, and review it every quarter.

✅ Give internal audit (in-house or outsourced) a direct line to the owners or the board.

In your business, who checks that the controls actually work?
