**Forming a Tax Group does not erase earlier intra-group tax deductions.**

Here is a simple example of how Aggregated Financial Statements (AFS) treat an old impairment.

**The story in four steps:**
▶ **Year 1:** F LLC wholly owns G LLC and lends it money. They are separate taxpayers.
▶ **Impairment:** F LLC impairs the loan in its own accounts. Subject to the conditions, the loss is deductible for Corporate Tax.
▶ **Tax Group:** F LLC and G LLC then form a Tax Group. At formation, the loan is not eliminated in the AFS, because the loss was already deducted.
▶ **Reversal:** later, F LLC reverses the impairment. The reversal is taxable for the Tax Group, up to the loss previously deducted. After that, the loan is eliminated in the AFS.

**The principle:** a deduction taken outside the group can come back as income inside it.

The same thinking applies to other items that pre-date the group, so check them before joining entities together.

✅ Before forming a Tax Group, list intra-group loans, impairments and provisions between the members.

✅ Note which were already deducted for Corporate Tax.
